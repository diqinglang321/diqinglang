## Pacing and spoken script

### 01. What does this trial test? — 00:00–00:06

Randomisation and objective weighing support the programme comparison. Three issues qualify its interpretation.

### 02. A calorie gap emerged. — 00:06–00:18

At six months, reported calorie intake fell more with HKD: seven hundred and four versus four hundred and twenty-three daily. Both groups had calorie-restriction advice.

### 03. Two routes could explain it. — 00:18–00:34

Diet composition might affect weight through ketosis or other physiological effects. It might also reduce appetite and calorie intake, which then reduces weight. These pathways can overlap.

### 04. Eating less may be the pathway. — 00:34–00:46

Lower intake may therefore be a mediator: a step through which HKD works. This can contribute to the programme effect, rather than invalidate randomisation.

### 05. The programme is not the mechanism. — 00:46–00:58

But these pathways were not separately randomized. This limits mechanistic attribution, not automatically the programme comparison; an independent composition effect is not identified.

### 06. A prescription is not exposure. — 00:58–01:08

Next, the prescription limited net carbohydrate to fifty grams daily. Self-reported food diaries assessed intake, not metabolic state.

### 07. Who actually entered ketosis? — 01:08–01:20

Without reported blood or urine ketone measurements, we cannot tell who entered ketosis, or whether it persisted.

### 08. Weight loss cannot verify ketosis. — 01:20–01:30

Weight loss cannot verify ketosis either: lower calorie intake can also cause it. The result concerns assigned advice, not an isolated effect of verified ketosis.

### 09. Who stays in the picture? — 01:30–01:35

Third, eighty participants entered the trial.

### 10. Who stays in the picture? — 01:35–01:43

Only fifty-nine completed six-month measurements. Twenty-one outcomes were missing: twelve in HKD and nine in ERD.

### 11. What if the missing people differed? — 01:43–01:58

Consider those twelve missing HKD outcomes. Their weight changes are unknown. If they lost less than measured participants, the available responses would not represent everyone assigned.

### 12. Selection can change the comparison. — 01:58–02:12

Completer-only HKD loss could then look larger. Other dropout patterns could shrink the gap. This is potential attrition bias: randomisation does not necessarily protect a selectively observed subset.

### 13. An ITT label cannot restore weights. — 02:12–02:24

ITT was claimed, but missing-outcome handling is unclear. Group labels cannot restore missing weights. Continued weighing after diet cessation preserves outcome information; remaining missingness requires assumptions.

### 14. A result is not a full explanation. — 02:24–02:30

Programme-level evidence is useful, while mechanism and missing-outcome effects remain unresolved.
